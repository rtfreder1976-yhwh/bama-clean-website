## Why Immediate Response Matters in Athens

Soot is acidic. Within hours of a fire, it begins etching glass, corroding metals, and permanently staining porous surfaces — walls, ceilings, carpet, upholstery, wood. Delay isn't measured in days. It's measured in hours.

A kitchen fire in an Athens home that's treated within 24 hours might result in a $3,000 restoration job. The same fire treated 72 hours later — after soot has begun permanently bonding to surfaces — could be $8,000 or more because more materials have to be replaced rather than cleaned.

## What Fire & Smoke Restoration Actually Involves

This is not wiping down walls. Smoke damage restoration is a multi-step process:

**Emergency corrosion mitigation.** Within the first 24 hours, treated surfaces to stop acid soot from doing further damage. This is the single most time-sensitive step.

**Content inventory and pack-out.** Salvageable contents are documented, removed, and cleaned separately. Non-salvageable items are documented for your insurance claim.

**Structural cleaning.** Walls, ceilings, floors, cabinetry, and structural members are cleaned using dry sponges, chemical sponges, and HEPA-filtered equipment depending on the soot type.

**Deodorization.** Thermal fogging or ozone treatment reaches into wall cavities, HVAC systems, and porous materials where odor-causing smoke particles are trapped.

**HVAC cleaning.** Smoke travels through ductwork. An uncleaned HVAC system will redistribute odor throughout the home every time it runs.
