## Why Athens, AL Has Elevated Radon Risk

Limestone. Limestone County is named for it, and it's literally under your feet.

Limestone and carbonate rocks contain uranium. Uranium decays into radium, which decays into radon — a radioactive gas. Radon migrates upward through soil and enters homes through:

- Foundation cracks and penetrations
- Crawlspace openings and vents
- Sump pits and floor drains
- Block wall joints in basements

Limestone County has historically tested among the higher radon counties in Alabama. The EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L. Many Athens homes test above that without any visible signs — radon is colorless and odorless. The only way to know is to test.

## What Happens If Your Athens Home Tests High?

Above 4 pCi/L, the EPA recommends mitigation. Radon mitigation involves installing a sub-slab or sub-membrane depressurization system — essentially a pipe and fan that vents radon from under your home to the outside before it enters your living space.
