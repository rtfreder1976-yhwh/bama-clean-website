## What Affects Upholstery Cleaning Cost in Athens?

**Fabric type is the biggest variable.** Microfiber, polyester, and nylon are forgiving — they handle moisture well and dry fast. Natural fabrics like cotton, linen, velvet, and wool require low-moisture methods and more careful chemistry. The wrong treatment on natural fabric causes shrinkage, color bleed, or water rings. An IICRC-certified technician checks the fabric code before choosing a method.

**Pet damage.** Athens families with dogs know what a claimed sofa looks like. Urine that's soaked into the cushion foam requires enzyme pre-treatment to break the bond before extraction. Surface cleaning alone doesn't remove it — it masks it until it dries and the smell returns.

**Soil level and age.** A sofa that's been used daily for 5 years in a family with kids takes more time than one that's lightly soiled. More passes, more pre-treatment dwell time, higher cost.

**Size and configuration.** A 3-seat sofa and an L-shaped sectional with chaise are both "a sofa" — but the sectional is two to three times the surface area.

## Should You Clean or Replace?

Clean it. Almost every time.

A quality sofa costs $600–$3,000. Professional cleaning costs $100–$200. Even if the sofa needs two cleanings a year, you'd have to do that for 7 years before it approached the cost of replacement. And replacement doesn't solve pet odor — the new sofa will smell the same in 6 months.

The exception: foam that's broken down structurally, or fabric that's shredded. No amount of cleaning fixes a failing frame or worn-through fabric. But discoloration and odor? That's what we do.
