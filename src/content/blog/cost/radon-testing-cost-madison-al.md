Radon is a radioactive gas that forms naturally from uranium decay in soil and rock. It seeps into homes through foundation cracks, slab penetrations, and construction joints. It is the second leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after smoking.

## Is Radon a Real Concern in Madison?

Yes. Madison County is designated EPA Radon Zone 2, meaning the area has moderate-to-high predicted average indoor radon levels. This means you have a real probability of elevated radon in your home. It does not mean every home is above the action level -- it means you should test.

### Local Geology

Madison sits on the edge of the Valley and Ridge geological province. The underlying limestone and phosphatic shales can be higher in uranium content than many other soil types, which drives higher radon potential.

### New Construction

Modern homes are more airtight than older construction. Better insulation means less natural ventilation -- radon that enters the home accumulates to higher concentrations. Many newer Madison subdivisions have never been tested.

## The EPA Action Level

The EPA recommends radon mitigation for any home measuring **4.0 pCi/L or higher**. The average US indoor radon level is 1.3 pCi/L. Outdoor air typically measures around 0.4 pCi/L.

Between 2.0-4.0 pCi/L, the EPA recommends considering mitigation.

## How Radon Mitigation Works

The most common system for Madison homes is **sub-slab depressurization (SSD)**:

1. A hole is drilled through the slab in a low-pressure area (typically a utility closet or garage)
2. PVC pipe is routed from beneath the slab to outside the home (usually up through the structure and out the roof)
3. A small fan draws air from beneath the slab continuously
4. Radon-laden soil gas is vented to the exterior before it enters the home

The system runs continuously. Electricity cost: $5-$10/month. Fan life: 5-10 years.

## When to Test for Radon in Madison

**Buy or sell a home:** Radon testing is standard practice in real estate transactions in Madison. Buyers commonly request it as a condition of purchase.

**Never tested:** If you have lived in your Madison home for years and never tested, test now. Radon exposure is cumulative.

**After remodeling:** Finishing a basement or major foundation work can change radon entry pathways.

**After 2 years:** Long-term mitigation systems should be verified to still be working. Fan failure without inspection can leave a home unprotected.

## Professional vs. DIY Testing

**DIY Testing:** Short-term kits from hardware stores: $15-$30. Place the device, collect for 48-96 hours, mail to the lab, receive results in 1-2 weeks. Accurate enough for an initial screen.

**Professional Testing:** A certified radon professional places a calibrated electronic continuous radon monitor and provides a written report with chain-of-custody documentation. Required for real estate transactions in most cases.
