## Is Radon a Real Concern in Huntsville?

Yes. Madison County is designated EPA Radon Zone 2, meaning the area has moderate-to-high predicted average indoor radon levels based on geology, housing characteristics, and indoor measurement data.

### Local Geology

Huntsville sits at the convergence of the Valley and Ridge and Cumberland Plateau geological provinces. The underlying limestone and shale formations in this part of North Alabama can contain higher uranium concentrations than many other soil types, which drives elevated radon potential.

### Older Huntsville Homes

Huntsville has significant housing stock from the 1950s-1970s Space Race era. Older construction typically has more foundation penetrations and gaps that allow radon entry. Many of these homes have never been tested.

### Modern Tight Construction

Newer Huntsville construction in Hampton Cove, Jones Valley, and other growing areas is more energy-efficient and airtight. This reduces natural ventilation and allows radon to accumulate to higher concentrations.

## The EPA Action Level

The EPA recommends radon mitigation for any home measuring **4.0 pCi/L or higher**. The average US indoor radon level is 1.3 pCi/L.

## How Radon Mitigation Works

The most common system for Huntsville homes is **sub-slab depressurization (SSD)**:

1. A hole is drilled through the slab in a low-pressure area
2. PVC pipe is routed from beneath the slab to outside the home
3. A small fan draws soil gas from beneath the slab continuously
4. Radon-laden air is vented outside before it enters the home

Electricity cost: $5-$10/month. Fan life: 5-10 years.
